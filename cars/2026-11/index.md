<div class="car-note"><strong>Current working recommendation:</strong> start with a £5–7k petrol automatic in good condition. Only move towards £8–12k if the cabin, driving experience and history meaningfully improve. The £13–18k cars are a discretionary comparison, not the default budget.</div>

## What matters

- **A cabin worth getting into.** The old Audi TT S line's excellent interior and Virtual Cockpit are the benchmark; driver-facing instruments matter more than the size of a central touchscreen.
- **An enjoyable drive and some character.** The MINI's cool, low-key appeal is real, but its older centre-heavy dashboard is a concern.
- **Suitable for Devon and walking weekends.** Normal lanes, trailhead car parks, boots and rucksack. No need to buy an off-roader just to go hiking.
- **Automatic gearbox.** Petrol preferred unless unusually strong evidence favours a diesel.
- **Understated, not joyless.** Nice design without a showy performance-SUV image.
- **Whole-life cost.** Insurance after returning from abroad, provenance, maintenance, tyres, fuel and tax matter alongside purchase price.

<h2 id="buying">Non-negotiable buying checks</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Insurance first:</strong> these published 1–50 groups are indicative only. Get a live quote for the <em>exact registration</em>, with your UK address and true NCD history; a group does not predict your actual premium. Ask your UAE insurer for written claims/NCD history and confirm UK acceptance.</li>
<li><strong>Provenance:</strong> HPI-style history check, V5C, VIN, outstanding finance, recorded insurance write-offs and whether the car is a UK-market vehicle or import.</li>
<li><strong>Maintenance:</strong> service invoices rather than just a seller claim; MOT history and advisories; gearbox operation and maintenance where specified.</li>
<li><strong>Feature proof:</strong> photos of the actual driver's screen, working CarPlay and specified equipment. Digital dashboard, navigation and CarPlay are not interchangeable descriptions.</li>
<li><strong>Reserve:</strong> avoid spending the entire budget on the sticker price. Keep funds aside for insurance, tyres and unexpected repairs.</li>
</ol>
<div class="car-note"><strong>Learning from the first listing search:</strong> the 2017 MINI Cooper Seven at £6,499 in Egham was a declared <strong>Category N insurance write-off</strong>; the 2014 Totnes MINI at £6,995 was a <strong>Japanese import</strong>. Neither is a clean comparator for an ordinary UK-market MINI, even though their prices initially looked attractive. Do not treat those listings as recommendations.</div>
